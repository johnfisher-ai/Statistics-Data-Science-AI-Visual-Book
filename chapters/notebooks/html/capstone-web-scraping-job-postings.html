Step D1 to D3 · What we collected and what it is a frame for¶

The target population is every open data-analyst vacancy in the region this quarter. There is no register of vacancies, so the frame became whatever a public job board would show us.

   a contact address; no login walls bypassed; no personal data of named individuals retained.
ANALYSIS: de-duplicate to the vacancy, then compare against the official earnings benchmark to
   quantify what the frame and the disclosure rule did to the estimate.

Read D3 again, because it is the whole chapter. Every listing matching the search was captured, which makes this a census of the frame with nothing sampled. There is no sampling error whatsoever, and any confidence interval computed from these data would be either zero-width or meaningless.

That is exactly why scraped datasets are dangerous. The one error a statistician is trained to look for is the one error that is definitely absent, and the three that remain, coverage, duplication and non-disclosure, have no standard formula and produce no warning.

Step 2 · Parse failures are not missing data¶

A scraper reads whatever the page contains. When it fails, it fails loudly and the value has to be voided rather than trusted.

What this shows. Advertised pay is right-skewed, which is what pay always looks like, with a dense band of ordinary roles and a thin upper tail. Every published vacancy quotes a range rather than a figure, with a median width of 8,200 dollars, so the midpoint used from here on is something we constructed, since no employer stated it.

The composition block is the one to hold on to. This frame is mostly mid-level roles at small employers, and 86 percent of it was posted by the employer directly rather than through an agency. Whether that matches the market or only matches what this particular site carries is exactly the question the benchmark in Step 5 is for.

The last block is the one that decides the chapter. Disclosure runs from 91 percent of junior roles down to 33 percent of lead roles.

Step 4 · Salary disclosure falls as seniority rises¶

Twenty-eight percent of vacancies show no salary on any of their listings. If that were a coin flip it would cost precision and nothing else, and the table below shows it is far from one.

Disclosure depends on seniority, and seniority depends on pay. Junior roles publish a number 91 percent of the time; lead roles do so 33 percent of the time, and lead roles pay roughly twice as much. Seniority is known for every vacancy, so given seniority the blanks can be treated as missing at random, which is what makes the Step 6 reweighting possible. Whether higher-paying employers within a band also hide the number more often, which would be missing not at random, cannot be tested from the board.

Dropping the blanks without that adjustment, which is what every default does, therefore costs data and also removes the top of the distribution, pulling down the mean of what remains.

Step 6 · How much reweighting on seniority repairs¶

Some of the damage is structure we can see. Disclosure varies by seniority, and we know the seniority of every vacancy whether or not it published a salary. That makes seniority usable as a weighting variable, exactly as age was in Capstone 17.

                      for s in SEN})                                            # the region

The first correction reweights the disclosed salaries to the board's own seniority mix, which removes the disclosure filter's distortion and recovers 55 percent of the gap. The second reweights instead to the region's seniority mix, which also compensates for the senior roles that never reached the board, and recovers 77 percent.

What remains cannot be pinned down from the board. Candidates include higher-paying employers hiding the number within a band, vacancies within a band that never reached the board, and the benchmark itself, which measures employees' earnings while the scrape measures advertised bands. No weighting scheme built from the board can separate these.

If they had pointed in opposite directions the scraped mean might have looked correct while resting on two large offsetting errors, which is a more dangerous situation than a visibly wrong number.

Step 9 · Ethics, bias, and limits¶

  • Permission is not a technicality. robots.txt and the terms of service decide whether collection is allowed; a rate limit decides whether it is considerate. None of the three is enforced by your code working. The plan records what was checked, and a study that cannot state this should not be published.
  • Personal data has a different bar entirely. Vacancy text is corporate, but scraped pages routinely carry named recruiters, direct phone numbers and email addresses. This study retained none, and that is a design decision made before collection rather than a cleaning step afterwards.
  • The frame is a business artifact. Which vacancies appear on a public board is decided by hiring practice, agency contracts and cost. That is a selection mechanism nobody designed for research purposes and nobody documents.
  • State the population the data actually describes. Write "advertised salary bands, among publicly advertised vacancies that chose to publish one, on one board, during one quarter" in place of "data-analyst salaries in the region", because the longer sentence is the honest one.
  • Do not report a confidence interval as though it covered the real uncertainty. With a census of the frame the sampling error is zero, and the interval will be narrow and confident. Every error in this chapter lives outside it.
  • Scraped data ages badly. The board is a live system: postings expire, get edited and reappear. Two scrapes a week apart are two different frames, and comparisons across time confound real change with changes in what the board was showing.

The notebook carries the chain: the collection plan and its permissions, resolving 1,382 listings to 808 vacancies, voiding parse failures, the disclosure pattern by seniority, the comparison against an external benchmark, the reweighting that recovers about three quarters of the gap, the coverage shortfall it cannot reach, and the one variable that came through intact.
